# Exploration direction

Date: 2026-11-01. Status: approved full art/design reset. All six terrain recipes and modular vegetation are implemented. Aurelia retains its v2 landscape; the other five worlds use `worlds-v3`. Coordinated visual and performance checks are recorded in [the performance review](performance-review.md) and [release receipt](qa/survival-polish-2026-11-01.json). Earlier first-region captures below are historical evidence, not validation of the expanded scene. Publication status is recorded by the repository's GitHub Pages deployment workflow.

## Player experience

Walking through and exploring distinct worlds is the center of the game. Crafting should help the player make a journey, investigate a place, or reach somewhere new. The planet's geometry, habitats, animals, lighting, and interface should support that experience together.

The user's scope is to rebuild the assets, visual design, lighting, rendering configuration, and procedural approach. Existing art is not a design constraint. Selected simulation foundations remain: spherical topology and identity, edited column collision, persistence, input/movement, resource interactions, and bounded worker streaming. They are retained where useful to the new experience, not protected from necessary changes. The new landscape recipes now cover every world. The modular fauna renderer is shared, with environmental eligibility: the airless moon has no biological residents, and molten liquid cannot host fish or wandering land creatures.

Proposed loop: notice an interesting feature, choose a route, encounter a habitat, observe or interact, record a discovery, then use what was learned or collected to reach another destination. Flight extends this loop. A distant world should suggest a different experience before landing, and its surface should deliver that difference at walking scale.

The requested UI/UX overhaul is active implementation work. Its direction is an exploration interface: a clear view of the world, restrained navigation and interaction cues, readable discoveries, and crafting presented in the context of the journey. Visual completion requires review in the actual game, including touch and gamepad flows. A redesigned HUD alone does not complete the exploration loop or ecology work described here.

## Evidence and current limits

The local stabilization changes address specific defects: terrain color was multiplied twice; procedural shading used camera-relative coordinates as planet coordinates; packed material decoding disagreed with the mesher; distant water and terrain used intersecting meshes; repeated planet visits resampled unchanged far-terrain and ocean data. These are bounded rendering and scheduling corrections, not proof that every distance transition or stall is resolved.

The active far-terrain proxy follows the fine layer datum with a one-layer downward offset. A fully stitched near/middle/far representation remains follow-up work. First-visit sampling now prepares detached buffers in short cancellable slices; the previous planet stays active until a ready swap. The [performance review](performance-review.md) separates measured request, preparation-slice, swap, and presentation timings and retains the adverse runs. An assisted 16-minute Survival journey exercises all six worlds, earned crafting, hunting and cooking; it exposed a first-draw buffer burst. Bounded GPU priming addresses that burst, with subsequent physical-flight rechecks recorded separately.

The current `aurelia-v2` recipe covers the whole sphere with coast, meadow, woodland, wetland, highland and dryland fields. Six stable destinations have distinct land approaches and clearings: Shore Camp, Windseed Downs, Rainbough Basin, Reedwater Reach, Cairnback Ridge and Ochre Steps. The composed starter route remains within this landscape, with 0.625 m fine layers and limestone. Five new tree families and eight understory modules replace legacy Aurelia tree meshes globally. The new fauna kit supplies persistent bounded residents and authored body plans with planted foot contact, rather than the earlier three visual-only grazers. This is a procedural habitat/population implementation, not a simulated food web.

The explorer, held props and vehicles use a reproducible code-authored GLB; camp and pickup assets use cached authored geometry. Lighting includes a local sun shadow, radial sky fill, tone mapping and daylight star fading; fine surface grain is baked and sampled in stable planet coordinates. Versioned save and co-op room keys keep prior recipe data separate.

The composed starter habitat still occupies a 205 m chart-radius interior with a 320 m blend, now joining the new global Aurelia generator. It is no longer the boundary of the rebuilt world. The old in-path limestone arch and local herd are removed from the active renderer. Six new landmark assemblies sit beside destination approaches; they are visual landmarks and do not yet participate in collision, mining or structural support. Local plant size decreases over the outer 10 m of the 65 m plant window. Trees use simpler outer assemblies and bounded residency, but a continuous near/mid/far vegetation representation remains unfinished. Landmarks also have a bounded visibility window.

The expedition far-terrain proxy now uses the same quantized layer datum as fine terrain and sits one 0.625 m layer below it. A dry-coast safeguard keeps low shores above liquid; the legacy recipe alone retains its 12 m offset. This reduces the gap during streaming and distance changes without establishing a fully stitched LOD seam. Released fine chunks restore coarse coverage before the next draw; additions can still overlap the lower proxy until the next mask update. Clouds integrate both visible shell lobes within their fixed sample budget, with length-normalized extinction and softened vertical edges.

The orbital view has an explicit representation switch: fine chunks stop drawing at a surface-view distance of 1,000 m and return below 800 m, while their data and collision remain resident. The far proxy receives full coverage in the same frame. Water becomes opaque and converges to stable ripple roughness over 200–800 m, concealing detailed seabed ownership and suppressing subpixel wave variation. The far mesh's redundant polygon depth bias has been removed. The camera increases its near plane with viewing distance, limited by the nearby explorer/skiff, to retain depth precision at orbit. This is a bounded orbital solution, not a new middle-distance LOD system. The coarse orbital surface represents the seeded terrain; fine player edits and nearby vegetation are restored when returning to the detailed view, rather than being reproduced in that coarse mesh.

Player-seeded trees throughout Aurelia use the new species/age kit and core chopping state. Authored starter trees retain their placement and identity when seeded. Each planet uses a local nearest-first window admitting at most 192 detailed growth assemblies, 128 simpler outer assemblies and 600 undergrowth clumps. Chopping or editing compacts live instance slots immediately; it does not wait for terrain mesh regeneration. These are rendering limits, not limits on saved ecosystem tiles. Dense or distant growth can be omitted from the current window. Every planet now selects its own modules and community rules; legacy chunk vegetation is disabled for all active expedition recipes.

## Relevant design precedents

These are documented techniques, followed by proposed adaptations. Their performance results do not transfer automatically to this browser game.

| Primary source | Documented approach | Proposed adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [System Era on Astroneer, 2019](https://www.unrealengine.com/spotlights/how-system-era-softworks-leveraged-ue4-to-create-astroneer-s-wonderful-universe) | Replaced an upfront planetary actor hierarchy with on-demand layered terrain and object generation; separated biome/cave layers; used foliage tooling and shared materials. The director prioritized discovery over maintaining many survival meters. | Keep detailed work near the player and budget terrain, vegetation, and animal updates independently. Let the landscape and discoveries occupy attention; show hazard information when useful. |
| [Minecraft world generation, Microsoft/Mojang](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/minecraft/creator/documents/world-generation?view=minecraft-bedrock-stable) | Separates terrain, biomes, structures, features, sky, and entity spawning. Feature rules govern placement and clustering; reusable tree templates can be selected with weighted variation. | Give each region a habitat identity that jointly controls material, plants, animal eligibility, and atmosphere. Use coherent patches and clearings instead of unrelated per-tile random objects. |
| [Ghost Ship's cave-generation explanation, 2024](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/548430/view/4593196713081471258) | Combines authored room shapes and connections with constrained procedural variation. Biomes change surfaces, decoration, hazards, and lighting. Designers repeatedly play generated variants for traversal and visibility. | Author place recipes such as a sheltered grove, stepped shore, crater basin, cliff passage, and exposed ridge. Preserve readable routes and focal features as recipes vary over spherical tile neighborhoods. |

Minecraft's documentation describes Bedrock world generation and customization; it is not a specification for this engine. Ghost Ship's authored caves are not open planets. We should adopt their separation of responsibilities and playtesting discipline while retaining Goldberg topology and an original visual identity.

## World and art proposal

Two generated concept sheets establish a candidate direction: [worlds and walking-scale interface](concepts/exploration-worlds-v1.png) and [explorer proportions and movement](concepts/explorer-movement-v1.png). Their [full prompts and generation record](concepts/prompts.md) are retained. These are mock-ups, not implemented assets or approved final designs. The environment sheet is denser and more detailed than the measured runtime presently supports; composition, habitat relationships, silhouettes, and color are the useful targets. The character study is a starting point for the complete mesh, rig, and animation rebuild below.

Build each world from a limited, intentional family of landforms and habitats. Large shapes establish routes and silhouettes. Middle-scale features create places to visit. Close detail rewards walking slowly. Broad color, roughness, vegetation structure, sound, and animal behavior should agree about the habitat.

Vegetation should form communities: canopy, understory, ground cover, exposed ground, and shore transitions. Clearings and interrupted density are useful compositions. Fauna should start from habitat and behavior roles, with controlled variation inside body plans that animate well. A recognizable animal grazing, investigating, resting, or reacting gives variation a purpose. These behaviors are proposed scope, not evidence of a simulated food web.

Use the same persistent planet coordinates and habitat decisions across render distances. Keep high-frequency material detail near the camera, with mipmaps or explicit fading where appropriate. Compare a small shared normal/roughness atlas against shader-only detail. Broad color variation and wetness can remain procedural. More noise layers alone do not establish better materials.

## Runtime versus offline ownership

| Responsibility | Proposed owner | Decision boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goldberg identity, terrain cells, edits, collision, persistence | Runtime code | Must agree with player interaction and saved state. Generate and mesh with bounded worker jobs. |
| Habitat fields, placement, population eligibility | Runtime code | Deterministic seeds and tile neighborhoods choose where content belongs. Rendering residency does not define world existence. |
| Repeated tree and rock prototypes | Compare runtime construction with Kiln/offline baking | Prefer the representation that meets appearance and measured runtime budgets; share geometry/materials and batch instances where suitable. |
| Creature body and animation assets | Compare both approaches | Runtime owns behavior and locomotion. Authored forms/clips or procedural articulation must meet the same silhouette, deformation, and interaction requirements. |
| Landmarks and modular place components | Kiln/offline candidate plus runtime placement | Review a small reusable kit; runtime supplies terrain fit, routes, state, and interactions. A baked model does not certify traversal. |
| Fine material detail and atlases | Offline candidate | Bake reusable detail when it reduces measured cost without harming style. Runtime supplies lighting, broad variation, wetness, and distance response. |
| LOD selection, culling, residency, disposal | Runtime code | Authored tiers and export metadata need explicit consumer integration and visual checks. |

Kiln is an authoring/export candidate, not the default answer for every object. Runtime procedural construction is also an asset-production method and must be judged by the resulting quality and cost.

### Comparison gate: one tree and one creature

Use the same art briefs, dimensions, camera distances, lighting, animation requirements, and target population in both paths. Retain the current runtime path as a baseline. Produce one reviewed tree and one reviewed creature through each candidate path before committing a larger pack.

| Measure | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Silhouette and material readability | Matched stills and movement captures at walking, flight, and distant-view scales; owner review of forms, color, surface response, and transitions. |
| Draw calls, triangles, materials | Actual renderer counts in the same populated scene, including relevant passes; separate asset export estimates from measured frame counts. |
| Load and memory | Download bytes, decode/build time, upload/first-render stalls, geometry memory, decoded texture memory, and repeated-instance cost. Record cold and warm runs separately. |
| Animation and interaction | Tree wind/chop behavior; creature idle/move/react poses, foot contact, turning on the sphere, bounds, pivot/orientation, and collision/target agreement. |
| Editing cost | Timed change to tree crown/branch shape and creature proportions/gait, including rebuild/export/import and visual recheck. Keep source and exact revision identity. |

Choose per family after reviewing these results. A smaller file, fewer source meshes, a successful export, or a prettier isolated render is insufficient by itself. No winner is assumed, and this comparison has not yet been completed.

### Production choices and Kiln's actual boundary

“Authored” can mean code-authored. Baking code into a reviewed asset changes when construction happens; it does not remove procedural authorship or supply good anatomy automatically.

| Path | What it would produce | What must be demonstrated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Runtime construction | Code builds a reusable mesh, skeleton, weights, and motion data, then caches them. | Construction/upload cost, reproducible form, usable deformation, and bounded population variation. Rebuilding these every frame is unnecessary. |
| Build-time procedural authoring | A separate program generates a canonical body, rig, and clips as a versioned GLB. | Reproducible exports, editable source, target-loader fidelity, and an efficient change/review cycle. The game need not ship the authoring program. |
| Kiln authoring and baking | The same authored decisions pass through Kiln's source/revision, inspection, preview, and export workflow. | A qualified skin-capable export route, successful in-game import, and useful review iteration. Kiln's value must be demonstrated against direct authoring. |

Kiln has explicit building blocks: `createBoneChain` creates bones; `createSkinnedPart` binds supplied geometry using caller-authored four-influence weights; `createClip` records authored local transform tracks. `createJointChain` creates rigid nodes, not a deforming body. Rig recipes are scaffolds, not finished anatomy, automatic weights, locomotion, or IK. Skin-integrity inspection checks binding and the inspected pose; it does not certify the whole animation envelope or deformation quality. These contracts were checked through live Discovery and the [skinning SDK documentation](https://kilnstudio.tools/docs/sdk/#explicit-character-skinning).

The connected host reported `exporter: legacy` and `skinning: false`. The explicit Three exporter is the documented skin-preserving candidate and remains experimental. It is selected when generating the asset, through host startup configuration or an isolated CLI/library invocation; a runtime delivery profile does not change an existing GLB's converter. No Kiln host setting was changed and its renderer was not qualified for this asset. [Exporter selection and handoff](https://kilnstudio.tools/docs/engine-handoff/).

The [organic-authoring discussion, issue 134](https://github.com/instruktlabs/kiln/issues/134), concerns shape helpers, QA, and comparison examples. It is useful modeling context, not evidence of automatic character rigging or production-ready animal animation.

The implemented player follows the hybrid ownership: author the canonical mesh, rest skeleton, weights, sockets, and motion library offline; runtime controls behavior, blending, terrain adaptation, and residency. A matched comparison of the production paths above is still required before choosing how to deliver the rest of the cast.

### Player and creature motion plan

Start with a readable expedition character whose silhouette, outfit, pack, hands, and tools belong to the world brief. Use articulated hips, knees, ankles, shoulders, elbows, and wrists; give hands and pack stable named attachment sockets. Deform clothing and body where needed, while rigid equipment can attach to bones. Keep opaque shared materials wherever the design allows.

Author idle, walk/run, starts/stops, turning, jump/landing, gathering/pickup, and the swimming/seated-flight poses that the game actually uses. The physics controller remains authoritative: begin with in-place locomotion and couple playback to measured tangential speed. Blend transitions deliberately; upper-body action layering requires a compatible track subset rather than blindly combining full-body poses. Three provides clip playback, weights, and crossfades; those APIs do not choose a convincing motion set. [AnimationMixer](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/AnimationMixer.html), [AnimationAction](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/AnimationAction.html).

Prototype one quadruped alongside the player, with coherent standing, walking, turning, resting, and reacting. Validate a separate body plan before adding birds or swimmers. Share clips and geometry where compatible, but give independently posed animals their own skeleton state. `SkeletonUtils.clone` reconnects cloned bones while sharing geometry/material references; cleanup must respect that ownership. Constrain procedural variation to reviewed ranges. Changing limb proportions requires compatible rest/bind transforms and stride/contact tuning. [SkeletonUtils](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/module-SkeletonUtils.html).

### Spherical-gravity contact pipeline

The local [foot solver](../src/render/footIk.ts) and [ground probe](../src/world/groundContact.ts) follow this sequence for edited terrain columns. Supporting collision for placed structures and more extensive joint-limit/contact cases remain follow-up work; neither authoring tool supplies them automatically.

1. Advance authoritative movement in persistent planet coordinates. Orient the rig to radial up and a continuous tangent heading, preserving orientation through turns and planet movement.
2. Evaluate the base animation and authored stance/swing weights. Identify nearby supporting terrain using the edited column collision representation, probing along local gravity and respecting cave floors. A global downward ray or the highest terrain column alone is insufficient around spherical worlds, caves, and overhangs.
3. Keep planted-foot targets in persistent planet coordinates. Convert them through the same floating-origin offset and rig transform as the character. This prevents a camera-origin shift from moving a planted foot.
4. Apply bounded pelvis correction and foot alignment, then solve the legs after clip evaluation, with current world matrices. Limit reach and release stale or unsupported contacts, blending by stance weight. Disable ground correction during jumping, swimming, and flight. More complete anatomical joint constraints are not yet implemented.
5. Refresh affected transforms and render. IK modifies the visual pose; it does not move the authoritative player capsule or manufacture collision support.

The local implementation uses an analytic two-bone leg solve with a forward knee bend plane. Three's `CCDIKSolver` remains an alternative: it operates on `SkinnedMesh` bone chains and exposes iteration limits, joint constraints, and blending, but does not supply a gait planner, terrain queries, planted-foot tracking, or pelvis behavior. No comparative speed or quality win against CCD has been measured. [CCDIKSolver contract](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/CCDIKSolver.html).

### Performance and acceptance boundaries

Cache asset construction, decoded geometry, materials, and clip actions. Schedule preparation/upload before an encounter. Update contact solving for nearby visible grounded actors; use tested pose-update rates and simpler representations farther away while keeping behavior independent of rendering. Ordinary mesh instancing does not by itself provide independent skeleton animation. Set bone, material, triangle, and population budgets from the matched prototype measurements rather than claiming that baking guarantees faster frames.

Measure animation evaluation, contact queries, IK, matrix updates, upload, and rendering separately at the intended population. Avoid per-frame allocations and full mesh scans where a reviewed conservative animation envelope can provide correct bounds. Three's skinned-mesh bounds need explicit attention as poses change. Stop and uncache mixers on removal, and release shared resources only when their owners are finished. [SkinnedMesh bounds and binding](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/SkinnedMesh.html), [AnimationMixer lifetime](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/AnimationMixer.html).

The next character gate is wider motion review, followed by the quadruped prototype and matched production comparison. Review intermediate bent poses, tool grip, foot sliding, knee popping, and silhouette at play distance. Extend the bounded WebGPU route to pentagons, cliffs, caves, platforms, low gravity, and WebGL2 motion. Rest and animated poses are imported in CPU regressions; those checks do not establish every rendered terrain case. Report cold load and warmed populated traversal separately. Passing export validation or showing a polished isolated render does not complete this gate. [GLTFLoader scene and clip delivery](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html).

Each species shares module geometry with other instances. Materials are opaque and front-sided. Crown and understory motion use one time uniform and deterministic per-instance wind phases, projected into the local tangent plane. Minerals and airless-world growth remain still. ## Implemented resident fauna kit (local)

The new [`faunaKit.ts`](../src/render/faunaKit.ts) is a reusable source kit with explicit body profiles, palettes, head sockets, ear/horn/tail modules, limb lengths, gait phases and foot sizes. Further species are authored by extending these reviewed profiles and rig recipes, not by claiming arbitrary combinations will animate correctly.

[`AureliaFauna`](../src/render/aureliaFauna.ts) works on Aurelia and the other terrain archetypes. Stance feet remain anchored in persistent planet coordinates; swing feet follow a lifted path toward the next contact; bounded body lowering accommodates reach. Segment lengths remain fixed. The fish uses tail/fins; the glider uses separate articulated solid wing membranes. Pools share geometry/material resources; no geometry is regenerated by the frame loop. These are implementation limits, not measured frame-time claims.
